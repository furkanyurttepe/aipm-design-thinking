## Session

- **Prototype:** v7 (`prototype/index.html`), on one phone passed around.
- **Learning question:** When newcomers play from a Jam Prompt Card, does the
  back-side question lead them to start and continue a conversation about the
  music after playing?
- **Setting:** One jam evening, ~2 hours. Observer: the AI PM.
- **Participants (anonymised):** N1 – guitar, moved 5 months ago · N2 – bass,
  moved 1 year ago · N3 – keys, moved 8 months ago · N4 – singer, moved 3
  months ago · plus regulars R1 and R2.
- **Task (neutral):** "Use this however you like during the next set." The back
  side and the corner were not explained.

## Limits of This Test

- Simulated; one evening; one observer who is also the designer.
- Only 4 newcomers, all players — **listeners were not tested.**
- Conversation lengths were estimated by the observer, not measured.

## Questions This Test Did Not Answer

- Does the conversation continue **after the event** (e.g. exchanging contacts)? *(E1)*
- Would the cards work without a regular (R1) helping to find the back?
- How do listeners (non-players) use the deck?

## Re-run After the Iteration

- **Checked (functional, automated):** over 900 dealt cards, prompt cards
  showed only M questions and Break cards only P questions; harder genres
  appeared about half as often (~23 vs ~44 each); the same genre never came
  twice in a row.
- **Reverted:** the dog-eared corner and its nudge (C2) were removed by the AI
  PM. The card again has no visual hint that it can be turned over, so
  "turned over" measures discoverability as well as the question (re-review
  finding 5). Open question: does turning the card over need a cue at all?
- **Not checked:** whether people talk longer after M questions. That needs a
  second test with people (optional extension "Run a Second Test").

was a regular who did it (T2-1, T2-2). Low confidence — two sessions.

**Next smallest change (AI PM decision):** add a standard turn-over symbol (↻)
to the front of each card — implemented in v9, then **removed in v11** by the
AI PM ("not needed"). Rejected alternative: the host telling people to turn
the card over (would lead the task). Open question remains: do people need a
cue to turn the card over?
