## Challenge

- **Person:** People who moved to a new city or region within the last ~2 years
  and who listen to and/or play music.
- **Goal:** Find someone they can talk to about music.
- **Scope (one journey):** From deciding to go to a music setting (jam, concert,
  event) to a music conversation starting — or not starting.
- **Starting assumptions:** See `practice-journey-map.md` (all assumptions).

## Open Questions (missing evidence)

## Persona

> **⚠️ SIMULATED evidence base.** Drafted by Claude from simulated notes.

**The Newcomer Music Fan**

- **Group and context:** People who moved to a new city within the last ~2
  years and who listen to or play music. *(E1–E8)*
- **Goal:** Find someone to talk to about music. *(Challenge)*
- **Behaviours:**
  - Goes to jams or concerts, sometimes alone. *(E2)*
  - Follows local venues online but does not contact strangers. *(E7)*
  - Has tried general friend-finding or event apps and abandoned them. *(E5)*
- **Pain points:**
  - Cannot tell who shares their music taste, at events *(E3)* or online *(E7)*.
  - Playing or listening together ends without a conversation. *(E1, O1)*
- **What seems to help:** A visible cue (e.g. a band T-shirt) *(E4)* or an
  introduction by a host at a jam *(E6, O2)* — *Interpretation: these give
  people a ready-made reason to talk.*
- **Open questions:**
  - Is P6's "online first" preference common or an exception? *(E8)*
  - Does the problem happen more during events or after them?
  - Do newcomers also go to concerts alone? *(E2 covers jams only.)*

## Point of View

> *SIMULATED evidence base.*

Newcomers who love music **need** a low-pressure opening to start a music
conversation, and a way to continue it, **because** being side by side at a
music event rarely turns into conversation on its own *(E1, E3, O1)*, while in
a few observed cases a visible cue or an introduction did *(E4, E6, O2)*.
